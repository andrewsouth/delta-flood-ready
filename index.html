Delta Flood Ready Toolkit

What the rural flood early-warning pilot looks like on the ground in Delta, Utah: the tools, spaces, and places where the work happens, and mockups of the public brief, the map, the program website, and the library's disaster corner. The example is a Sevier River snowmelt year at the watch tier.

These are illustrative mockups. Every flow, date, percentage, and map zone is invented for the design and is not a real forecast. Program names and web addresses are placeholders.

How the pieces connect

Messages move left to right, from forecasts to households, and pass through one human approval point. Information comes back along the bottom into the community layer, where the program uses it and agencies above the community can pull it.

Tools, spaces, and places

The work happens in three kinds of settings. Tools are the data and software that produce and handle information. Spaces are the shared channels (digital and social) where messages travel. Places are the physical locations where people meet the program in person.

Tools data and software

  • Forecast sourcesGEOGLOWS ensembles, the National Water Model, CBRFC river and seasonal forecasts, NWS watches and warnings, NRCS snowpack, USGS gauges
  • The community layerArcGIS Survey123 for observer reports and the pulse survey; ArcGIS Hub for the website; ArcGIS Dashboards at three levels
  • The AI agentDrafts the brief and deeper look in English and Spanish, summarizes reports, and flags gaps for the custodian to approve
  • Message toolsText-list service, QR codes and short links for each channel, a print template for the brief
  • Backup gearNOAA Weather Radios, ham radio (ARES), staff gauges painted on bridge piers

Spaces shared channels

  • Program websiteThe public brief, the deeper look, maps, and the report form (Layer 1 and Layer 2)
  • Text listEnglish and Spanish lists, sent at outlook, watch, and warning
  • Relational channelsWard emails and ministering contacts, congregation group chats, town Facebook groups, phone trees
  • BroadcastThe local paper, regional radio, school text alerts
  • Observer networkA shared Survey123 group and a monthly digest for trained Layer 2 members

Places physical locations

  • Library disaster cornerStanding education, the current brief, sign-ups, and plan nights (the anchor place)
  • Extension officeTraining sessions, workshops, and the custodian's desk for the community layer
  • Ward buildings and churchesAnnouncements, sandbag crew sign-ups, and possible refuges
  • Fire stationEquipment staging, drills, and a brief posted on the door
  • Everyday stopsFeed store, grocery, gas stations, and the post office, each with its own QR code on a posted brief
  • Custodial sitesSchools, care centers, and farms with worker housing, each with its own trigger checklist

The one-page public brief

Flood watch
Brief 7, Wednesday, May 6
The Sevier River is rising and may reach flood level by Saturday.

What this means for you

  • Fields and roads near Gunnison Bend and north of Main Street may flood.
  • The river road crossing north of town may close this weekend.
  • Town streets south of Main are not expected to flood.

What to do now

  • Move equipment, stock, and vehicles out of the shaded areas.
  • Check your family plan and your neighbors' plans.
  • Sign up for flood texts at the library or by the QR code.

Pueden inundarse los campos y caminos cerca de Gunnison Bend y al norte de Main Street. Ahora: mueva equipo, animales y vehículos fuera de las zonas sombreadas, revise su plan familiar y regístrese para recibir mensajes de texto.
Official source: National Weather Service Salt Lake City flood watch, repeated word for word at the QR link, with Millard County Emergency Management. This brief adds local detail only. In an emergency, call 911. Scan for the deeper look and to report conditions.

How it's built

  • Same layout at every tier. Only the tier ladder, chip color, headline, map shading, and actions change, so people learn to read it in the slow months.
  • One headline in plain words. No river stages or flows on the public page; those live in the deeper look.
  • Two short columns: "what this means for you" and "what to do now."
  • Spanish on the same page, not on a separate sheet.
  • Official source named, so the brief always echoes the NWS and the county.
  • A QR code for each channel. The copy at the feed store gets a different code from the one at the library, which measures reach.

The map

The brief's map is deliberately simple: the river, the reservoirs, the canals, the town, the roads that matter, and three shaded zones. It names places people know (Main Street, Gunnison Bend, the river road crossing) and shows where the library, fire station, and higher ground are. The website version below lets readers move between flow levels.

The map is a schematic drawn for this mockup. It is not to scale, and the zones are not real inundation estimates. A working version would draw on NWM or GEOGLOWS-based flood inundation mapping, checked against local records of past floods (such as the documented 1983 flood).

The library disaster corner

A standing space of about 12 by 16 feet in the Delta City Library. It works year-round as an education space and turns into the town's flood information point as the tiers rise. Nothing in it is expensive: a bulletin wall, a map table, a shelf, a rack, a tablet, and a few chairs.

1

"The Sevier Now" wallTier light, this week's brief in both languages, and a season chart of snowpack and river flow that staff update at each brief.

2

Map tableA laminated flood map of Delta under glass, with markers so families can find their home, their route out, and their meeting place.

3

Ready shelfA model go-bag, sandbag how-to cards, and a "documents to copy" checklist. Display only; the take-home versions are on the rack.

4

Weather radio demoA NOAA Weather Radio set up to show how alerts sound, with a sign about where to get one.

5

Take-home rackCopies of the current brief, family plan templates, alert sign-up cards, and Layer 2 training flyers, in English and Spanish.

6

Sign-up tabletText-list sign-up, the pulse survey, and Layer 2 volunteer interest. Library staff can help at the desk.

7

Plan-night seatingFolding chairs for monthly plan nights, Extension workshops, and Layer 2 training, facing the wall display.

8

Kids' nookPicture books on weather and floods and a "pack your own go-bag" activity, so families come together.

Sources the deeper look links to

In a real build, each would link to the live product for the Sevier basin: NWS Salt Lake City watches and warnings; Colorado Basin River Forecast Center river and water-supply forecasts; NRCS snow survey and SNOTEL; USGS streamflow gauges; GEOGLOWS River Forecast System; and the National Water Model. Links are left out of this mockup on purpose.
